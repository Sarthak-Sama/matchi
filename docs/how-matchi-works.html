Underlying logic, in plain language

How Matchi decides where you should live

Matchi helps one person choose a place to live in Tokyo's 23 wards. It does not rank areas with one fixed score. It keeps a set of guesses about what you value, measured in yen per month, and narrows those guesses every time you say or tap something.

The one rule everything else serves

The language model never states a number, an area fact or a ranking of its own. Each one comes from a tool result in the same turn, and the harness checks that the reply matches.

One turn, in five steps

  1. ListenThe model turns what you said into typed evidence items.
  2. ReweightEach item reweights about 4,000 sampled versions of you.
  3. ValueEvery area gets a monthly yen value under every version.
  4. ShortlistPick the five areas that, as a set, serve the most versions of you.
  5. Ask or stopReport how much is still at risk. If it is large, ask. If small, commit.

Three properties hold throughout. Every area fact comes from the database. Every quantity is in yen per month or minutes, so any trade-off can be said in plain words. A fixed random seed means the same evidence always gives the same shortlist.

Stop 1 · Where the facts live

The database and how everything connects to it

The engine does no routing and no SQL. Everything it knows about Tokyo is one table of 937 rows that the database prepares in advance.

How the running app touches the database

At startup, once

  • The API opens a Postgres pool.
  • loadFeatureStore reads all 937 rows of agent.area_features and model_params into memory.
  • The rail graph (peak and off-peak) loads from rail_edges.

When you set an anchor

  • You name a workplace or school (a station or a point).
  • The API runs a reverse shortest-path search from that spot over the in-memory graph.
  • Result: one-way minutes from every locality to it. Unreachable ones get NaN.
  • These go into engine.setProfile. The engine never routes itself.

On every turn

  • No database round trip. The engine works on arrays in memory.
  • The only exception is query_areas, an optional SQL tool, off unless AGENT_QUERY_AREAS=1. It runs read-only with a 5 second timeout.
How travel time to a locality is estimated

For each of a locality's 9 sample points, take the best of its 3 nearest stations: walk to the station (straight-line distance × 1.3 at 80 m per minute), ride, then walk at the other end. Rail time includes a 4 minute wait at peak (6 off-peak) and 5 minutes per transfer. Take the median across the 9 points. Peak means arriving 07:30 to 10:00.

Stop 2 · The maths, part one

What an area is worth to you, in yen a month

Every area gets one number: what living there is worth to a particular version of you. Amenities add. Rent, travel time and broken soft limits subtract.

Uθ(a) = −R(a) − cT·T(a) + Σj wj·sj(xaj) − λB·[R(a) − B]+ − γ·cT·[T1(a) − TC]+ θ is one version of you (a full set of preferences). A bracket with a plus sign means "zero if negative".
SymbolIn plain wordsUnit
R(a)Modelled monthly rent for your chosen layoutyen
T(a)Hours per month you would spend travelling, across all your anchorshours
cTWhat one hour of your travel time costs youyen per hour
xaj, sjHow much of feature j the area has, and a curve that squashes it into 0 to 1none
wjWhat having plenty of feature j is worth to you compared with none. Can be negative (noise)yen per month
B, λBYour budget, and how much extra each yen over it hurtsyen, ratio
T1, TC, γHours to your main anchor, the hours your stated commute cap allows, and an extra cost multiplier on time beyond the caphours, ratio

A firm limit is not a penalty. Areas that break it are removed before scoring. Only soft limits become the two penalty terms.

Rent

R(a) = rward · m(a) · Alayout + fward,   m(a) = clip( (La / L̃ward)ε, 0.7, 1.4 )

Start with the ward's rent per m², scale it by the layout's size (a 1K is about 24 m²), add the ward's management fee. Then shift it for the locality: L is the local land value and L̃ the median land value in the ward. A locality with pricier land gets a higher rent multiplier. The exponent ε is 0.3555, fitted across the 23 wards. The multiplier is clipped to 0.7 to 1.4 because 129 localities sat on the old 0.85 to 1.15 limit.

Travel time

T(a) = (52 / 12) · Σm nm · 2tm(a) / 60

For each anchor m: round trips per week n times one-way minutes t, doubled for the return, divided by 60, then scaled from weeks to months (52/12). Each anchor reads its own period, so a daytime school and a late shift can differ.

Saturation: more helps less and less

s(x) = 1 − 10−x / h

h is the amount that counts as "enough". At x = h the curve is at 0.9. A neighbourhood with 4 supermarkets is nearly as good as one with 20, unless you say otherwise.

    Rent and travel time are guesses, so the engine treats them as guesses

    Ri(a) = R(a)·eσR z,   ti(a) = t(a) + max(3, 0.15·t(a))·z

    Each sampled version of you sees slightly different rent and travel times (z is a random draw from a bell curve, fixed for the whole session). σR is 8% when a land-price point is within 300 m and 12% otherwise.     Stop 3 · The maths, part two

    The belief: 4,000 plausible versions of you

    The engine never knows your true preferences. It holds 4,000 guesses, each a full set of numbers (cT, the twelve w values, λB, γ). Each guess has a weight. All weights start equal. Evidence changes the weights, never the guesses.

    Where the guesses start (the prior)

    Every yen figure is drawn as a fraction of your budget B, so one prior serves a ¥70,000 student and a ¥250,000 couple. With no budget given, the engine uses the median modelled rent of the reachable areas instead.

    ParameterHow it is drawnWhy
    cT (cost of an hour)(B / 133) × archetype factor × lognormal(0, 0.7)Rent is about a third of take-home pay, about 160 paid hours a month, travel valued near 40% of hourly pay. At ¥100,000 the median is about ¥750 per hour
    wj (one-sided)B × (archetype percent / 100) × lognormal(0, 0.8)Wide on purpose. The sign is fixed by the feature
    wj (bars, either sign)Normal, mean from archetype, spread 3% of BSome people like bars, some avoid them
    λBlognormal around 1A yen over budget hurts about twice a yen under it
    γlognormal around 3Minutes beyond the cap cost 3 times extra

    Each guess is also assigned one of five archetypes at random (student, young professional, couple, family, remote worker), which sets where its medians sit. For example a family guess values green space at 5% of budget and a student guess at 1%. These tables are hand-set starting points. They only need to be plausible and wide, because the first two or three pieces of evidence move the weights to the right mixture.

    How evidence moves the weights

    πi ← πi · ( (1 − ε)·Le(θi) + ε )αe,   ε = 0.05 Then divide by the total so the weights sum to 1. π is a guess's weight. L is how likely your statement would be if you were that guess. α is strength: 0.5 weak, 1 normal, 2 strong.

    A guess that would have said what you said gains weight. One that would not loses weight. The 0.05 floor assumes a 5% chance any statement was misheard, so one odd remark never wipes out the belief.

    Try it: watch a belief learn

    One question only: how much do you value having bars nearby? Each dot is one of 80 guesses (the real engine uses 4,000 over twelve features). Bigger dot means more weight. Add evidence, then remove an item to see the replay.

    Notice that "matters a lot" alone makes two clusters, one liking bars and one avoiding them. It says how big, not which direction. Adding a direction resolves it.

    Replay, not accumulation

    The belief is always the prior multiplied by the likelihoods of the items currently in the log. Add, edit or delete an item and the engine recomputes from the prior. So order never matters, and deleting a chip on the page exactly undoes it. The log, with the words you used, is the whole memory of the session.

    How healthy is the belief?

    ESS = 1 / Σi πi2

    The effective sample size says how many guesses still carry weight. It starts at 4,000. If it drops below 200 the design redraws a fresh set of guesses from the current belief. That redraw is not built yet: for now the page shows a low-confidence badge. The page shows, for each parameter, the weighted median and the 10th to 90th percentile range as a share of budget. That range narrowing is learning, made visible.

    Stop 4 · Turning words into maths

    Evidence: nine kinds of statement

    The language model's job is to translate what you say into one of nine typed items. Each type has a fixed likelihood formula in the engine. The model never touches the maths.

    TypeYou might sayHow likely if you were guess θ
    prefer_area"I'd take Koenji over Nakano"logistic of (U(a) − U(b)) / β
    pick_from_setYou tap one of five cardsexp(U(c)/β) divided by the sum over the cards
    reject_area"Not that one"1 minus the chance you would pick it
    direction"I hate noise"logistic of sign × wj / τ
    magnitude"Green space is essential"high if |wj| is near the tier size
    compare_importance"Quiet matters more than food"logistic of (|wj| − |wk|) / τ
    time_tradeoff"I'd pay ¥10,000 more to save 15 minutes each way"cT is above or below the hourly rate the sentence implies
    like_area"Somewhere like Shimokitazawa"q(x)², where q is the area's percentile rank on everything except rent
    dislike_area"Nothing like Roppongi"(1 − q(x))²
    • Noise scales. β is 3% of budget for choosing between areas, τ is 1% for statements about weights.
    • Magnitude tiers are low 1%, medium 2%, high 4%, essential 8% of budget. They double each step, matching the old importance values 1, 2, 4, 8.
    • Reactions to cards. "Too far" or "too suburban" become weak time_tradeoff or direction items.
    • Facts about your life are not evidence. Where you go, how often, layout, budget and commute cap (firm or soft) go into the profile. Firm limits filter areas. Soft ones become penalty terms.
    • "But cheaper" needs no evidence at all, because the rent term already rewards it.

    Surprise: noticing when you contradict yourself

    pe = Σi πi · ( (1 − ε)·Le(θi) + ε )

    Before applying an item, the engine asks how expected it was under the current belief. Below 0.15 it flags the item as surprising and names the earlier item it clashes with most (the one whose removal would make the new item more expected). The agent then says it out loud: "you said budget is firm, but you keep preferring places above it." Magnitude, like and dislike items are exempt from the flag.

    Stop 5 · Choosing what to show

    The shortlist: five areas chosen as a team

    Taking the five highest-scoring areas would give five near-copies. Instead the engine picks the set of five that gives the highest expected value when you choose your own favourite among them.

    EUS(S) = Σi πi · maxa ∈ S Ui(a)

    Algorithm. Take the feasible areas (those passing every firm limit with usable travel data). Start with an empty set. Five times, add the area that raises EUS the most. A near-duplicate of an area already chosen adds almost nothing, so the list diversifies by itself: each area is there because it is the best answer for some share of the plausible versions of you. This greedy method is guaranteed to reach at least 63% of the best possible set.

    Cost. Utilities sit in a 4,000 × 937 array. One shortlist is about 19 million operations. Measured on the real data, recommend takes 32 to 55 ms.

    What each card carries

    Pbest(a) = Σi πi · 𝟙[a is best for θi],   Regret(a) = Σi πi ( maxb Ui(b) − Ui(a) )
    OutputMeaningShown as
    Chance of being bestShare of plausible versions of you for whom this area wins"Top pick in 41% of plausible versions of you" (an example)
    RegretYen per month lost on average by choosing it over the true best"About ¥3,000 a month from ideal" (an example)
    RoleThe one parameter that most distinguishes the versions of you where this card wins"Best if your time matters most"
    BreakdownEach term of U in yen, compared with the median feasible areaRent, time cost, each amenity
    ConfidenceHow trustworthy the inputs areA plain label per number
    Two utilities, on purpose

    The shortlist, risk and regret use the noise-free utility, so rent and travel noise cannot put a floor under the risk number. The noisy utility is used only for Pbest (over the top 20 areas) and for "a beats b" in comparisons.

    Stop 6 · Knowing when to stop

    Ask another question, or commit

    The engine asks only while an answer could change the recommendation by an amount worth your time, measured in yen per month.

    Risk(S) = Σi πi ( maxb ∈ F Ui(b) − maxa ∈ S Ui(a) ) The average amount you lose by having only this shortlist to choose from, instead of every feasible area F.

    The agent can then say something plain, such as "what I still don't know about you is worth under ¥1,000 a month", using whatever figure the engine reports.

    The stop rule as built

    • Stop when six questions have been asked, or when risk is under 1% of budget and at least two questions have been asked.
    • Also stop if no question can be formed (no missing fact and fewer than two shortlisted areas).
    Why the minimum of two questions exists

    The design said "stop when risk is under 1% of budget". On real data the risk was only ¥70 to ¥300 (under 0.3% of budget) from the very first turn, even though the top area won in only 9 to 14% of samples. So the engine would have never asked anything. The rule now needs at least two questions first. Risk is still reported truthfully.

    Which question comes first

    1. A missing profile fact: anchor, layout or budget. Nothing else can be asked well without these.
    2. The shortlist itself: "Which of these feels closest?" Reading the tap is the default question. Showing the set that maximises EUS is also close to the most informative thing to show (a result from the preference-elicitation research).
    3. A pair of real areas: the pair from the shortlist whose chance of "a beats b" is closest to one half, among areas with Pbest above 0.05. If fewer than two qualify, the top two by expected utility.
    EVOI(q) = Σo P(o) · maxa E[U(a) | o] − maxa E[U(a)] The full "expected value of information" formula. It is designed but not built. The pair rule above is the shortcut used instead.

    Questions always use real areas and real figures, never abstract sliders: "Koenji at ¥82,000 and 38 minutes, or Kami-Igusa at ¥74,000 and 52?" (placeholder figures).

    Stop 7 · Reasons you can check

    Explanations come from the same numbers

    Every explanation is computed from the same samples and utilities as the recommendation, so it cannot disagree with it. The agent only phrases what these functions return.

    Why this area

    The yen breakdown at your average parameters, with the two biggest gains and two biggest losses against the median feasible area. Shape: "¥8,000 a month cheaper than typical for you, 5 hours a month more travel."

    Why not area X

    1. If X breaks a firm limit, say which and by how much.
    2. If X wins for at least 1% of the plausible versions of you, name the parameter that matters most for them.
    3. Otherwise say the smallest change that would make X win: the weight on one term would need to grow by some factor.
    4. If X is no better on anything you value, say so.

    Relax

    For each limit, try fixed steps (budget +5%, +10%, +20%; commute cap +5, +10, +15 minutes). Report how many areas become feasible and how much the best expected value rises, in yen. If nothing fits, you get these options and never just "nothing found".

    Compare A and B

    The term-by-term yen difference at your average parameters, plus the chance that A beats B across all samples.

    If I'm wrong about you

    One extra card: among the samples where the top pick is not best, the area that wins most often, and the parameter that distinguishes those samples.

    Stop 8 · The agent around the model

    The agent harness

    The harness is the code in the API that sits between the page, the model and the tools. It is the only part that talks to all three. The model reaches the engine and database only through a tool call that the harness validates, runs and records.

    The nine tools

    ToolWhat it doesPermission
    lookupFind an area, station or place by name. Up to 5 matches. Every id used elsewhere comes from hereRuns freely
    set_profileStore anchors, layout, budget, commute cap (each firm or soft). Reports refused anchors and the count of feasible areasRuns, always shown
    observeAdd evidence items with their quoted words. Returns how surprising each was and what it conflicts withRuns, always shown as a chip
    remove_evidenceDelete an item, the belief replays. The page can call this directlyRuns, always shown
    recommendThe shortlist, risk, stop flag and next question. The only source of rankingsRuns freely
    why_notThe four-step "why not X" procedureRuns freely
    relaxWhat loosening each limit would gainRuns freely
    compareTerm-by-term difference of two areasRuns freely
    query_areasOne SQL SELECT against the agent schema, up to 50 rows. Off by defaultSandboxed

    Each tool checks its input with a schema and returns compact JSON. Errors come back as typed results the model can act on, never as exceptions. Figures are rounded before they are returned: rent to the nearest ¥1,000, minutes to whole minutes, probabilities to whole percents. Every figure carries its unit and label, such as {"rent_yen": 82000, "rent_label": "modelled, ward-level"}. Place names from OpenStreetMap could contain anything, so free text travels in a field called untrusted_text and the model is told never to follow instructions found there.

    One turn through the loop

    1. LoadSession: profile, evidence log, seed, recent turns.
    2. BuildSystem prompt, state summary, last 8 exchanges, your message.
    3. CallThe model, with tool definitions.
    4. Run toolsValidate, run, record. Up to 6 calls per turn.
    5. Auto-recommendAfter any profile or evidence change, the harness runs recommend itself. A stale shortlist never reaches the page.
    6. GroundCheck every figure in the reply against this turn's tool results.
    7. StreamSend the state, then done, and save the turn.

    Events sent to the page: text, tool_call, tool_result, state, grounding, done, error. As built, each model answer is checked first and sent as one text event, so a failed draft never reaches the page.

    The grounding check

    Collectevery number in this turn's tool results and your profile
    →
    Extractevery number in the reply that carries a unit: yen, minutes, percent, a count of areas
    →
    Matcheach must equal a collected value after the same rounding
    →
    On a missone correction message and a second answer. If it fails again, the bad sentence is dropped and the cards are shown
    Known gap

    The check matches a figure against any collected number. A real figure attached to the wrong label (for example a rent quoted as a commute time) would pass. It catches invented figures, not mislabelled ones.

    Memory: the profile and the evidence log, not the transcript

    TierHoldsLifetime
    Session stateProfile, evidence log with your quotes, random seedThe session. The belief is recomputed, never stored
    Conversation windowLast 8 exchanges, without old tool resultsRolling
    Tool resultsFull JSON of this turn's callsThis turn only
    ReferenceColumn comments from agent.area_features, the feature list, the evidence typesLoaded at startup, part of the system prompt

    Every turn the model also gets a state summary of under about 400 tokens: your profile, each evidence item as type, quote and strength, the widest and narrowest belief ranges, the current shortlist with chances, risk, and questions asked. Normally a long chat forces a lossy summary. Here the evidence log is a lossless record of what was learned, each item tied to your own words, so old turns can drop out and only tone is lost. You can see every item as a chip and delete it.

    Permissions and limits

    What the agent can do

    • Read and reason freely.
    • Change only its own session state, and every change shows as a chip you can delete.
    • No write tools against the database and no network tools.
    • It can produce a listings-site link, but nothing is opened or fetched by the agent. You click.

    The SQL guard for query_areas

    1. One statement, starting with SELECT or WITH.
    2. No semicolons, comments or write words.
    3. Only tables and functions in the agent schema.
    4. Wrapped as SELECT * FROM (…) q LIMIT 50.
    5. Run in a read-only transaction with a 5 second timeout.

    The guard is pattern-based, so it is the first line. A read-only database role is meant to be the second, and is not wired in yet.

    Budgets

    • 6 tool calls per turn
    • 4 model calls per turn, including one grounding retry
    • 50 rows per query
    • 40 turns per session
    • 20 requests per minute on the turn route

    The model connection

    One interface, one implementation that speaks the OpenAI-compatible chat format with tool calling. Three environment variables choose the provider: AGENT_MODEL_BASE_URL, AGENT_MODEL_API_KEY, AGENT_MODEL. A scripted fake model with the same interface is used in tests. With no model configured, the page still works in engine-only mode: buttons add evidence directly (quieter, cheaper, closer, tap a card), and the shortlist, belief panel and chips all update. That is also the fallback if the model API fails.

    Stop 9 · Does it work?

    Three layers of testing

    LayerQuestionNeeds a model?Status
    Invariant testsIs the engine correct?Nobuilt · 67 engine tests pass
    Simulated usersDoes asking questions beat the fixed form?Nobuilt · form baseline missing
    Scripted personasDoes the whole agent behave?Yeswritten · not run live

    Ten invariants that must always hold

    • Weights are non-negative and sum to 1 after every operation.
    • With no evidence, the belief equals the prior.
    • Adding then removing an item restores the earlier weights exactly.
    • Applying the same items in any order gives the same weights.
    • No area that breaks a firm limit ever appears in a shortlist.
    • Pbest over the feasible set sums to 1.
    • Risk is never negative and does not rise when the shortlist grows.
    • Raising the budget never reduces the number of feasible areas.
    • The same seed, profile and evidence give an identical shortlist.
    • A "prefer a over b" item never lowers the chance that a beats b.

    Simulated users: regret falls as the engine asks

    Draw 200 hidden people from the prior, each with true preferences the engine cannot see. Each answers a shortlist by picking their true favourite, with the same choice noise the engine assumes. After 0 to 6 answers, measure the hidden person's true regret: the value of their real best area minus the value of the engine's top pick, in yen per month. This run used the real 937 areas (768 feasible), an anchor at Shinjuku, a 1K layout and a ¥100,000 soft budget.

    Scripted personas

    Seven short scripts replay against the live loop, with checks on the transcript: a student on a tight firm budget; school plus a late shift; two commutes in opposite directions; "like Shimokitazawa but cheaper"; the contradictor who says quiet is essential then picks the liveliest card twice; an anchor in Yokohama; and impossible limits (a 1LDK for ¥50,000 within 15 minutes). Checks on every transcript: the grounding check passes, no area breaking a firm limit is recommended, every tool call validates, and no turn goes over the tool budget.
